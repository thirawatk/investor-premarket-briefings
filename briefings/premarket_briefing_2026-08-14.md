## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

Let's be honest about what the numbers are telling us.

The S&P 500 at 7,798 with a Crestmont P/E of 43.8 is priced for perfection. The earnings growth is real — 24.7% is exceptional — but it's concentrated in a handful of mega-cap AI beneficiaries. Strip out the Magnificent Seven and the earnings picture looks far less impressive. The median stock is not growing earnings at 25%.

The consumer is weakening. UMich sentiment hit a record low of 47.6 in April 2026. The labor market is showing cracks with recent job losses. Retail sales may have beaten expectations last month (+1% vs +0.4% expected), but that's backward-looking. The forward picture — stagflation — is the nightmare scenario for equity multiples.

At a 35x P/E, Apple is pricing in 9-11% sequential revenue growth and successful AI monetization. The Jefferies downgrade to $264 (13.6% downside) is a useful contrarian anchor. The consensus target of $322 implies only 5.6% upside — not compelling risk/reward for a stock facing supply constraints and conservative guidance.

The uncomfortable math: if the Fed is forced to hike into a weakening economy because of persistent inflation, the Crestmont P/E mean-reverts from 43.8 to even 30 — that's a 31% drawdown. The market is priced as if the Fed can engineer a soft landing while fighting inflation caused by an energy supply shock. That's a narrow path, and the margin for error is zero.

**The bottom line:** This is a market that rewards momentum traders and punishes value investors. The earnings are real, but the price you're paying for them assumes nothing goes wrong. History says something always goes wrong.
