Fed funds futures (centralbank.watch, recalculated daily) now price **72bp of cumulative tightening — roughly 2.9 hikes — across the next 10 meetings**, with **77.2% odds of a move at the Dec 9 meeting** and an 89.1% cumulative probability that rates are *higher* by then. The 10Y closed at **5.045%** and the 30Y at **5.361%**. Every dollar of 2028–2030 earnings in a long-duration equity is now discounted at a rate that went up, not down, and the Fed's own statement says growth is "strong" — i.e. there is no macro excuse for the Fed to ride to the rescue.

## 🧠 The Hard Truth (Damodaran Lens)

The risk-free rate is 5.045% and SGOV pays you 4.06% to do nothing. That is the hurdle every stock in this book has to beat by a real margin, and it is now *higher* than it was in July — the market prices 72bp of further tightening, not cuts. Any equity you buy today is a claim that must clear roughly 9–10% to compensate for the risk you are taking over a Treasury bill; most of the names on this watchlist are being underwritten on the assumption that the discount rate falls. It isn't falling.

Look at what the market did to Fortinet: beat on EPS, beat on revenue, raised full-year revenue guidance for the third time — **and lost 25.9% of its market value in one after-hours session** because service revenue growth slowed from 26% to 20% and the service guide was cut $40M. That is the exchange rate the market is currently applying to decelerating growth, and it is the single most important number in this briefing. ZS at $255.80 is **65% above** the $150–155 zone you wrote down as an entry, sitting in exactly the same seat Fortinet was in at 4:05pm yesterday. If you are waiting for $150–155, you are waiting for a -40% drawdown; either that level is real and you should be prepared to buy a Fortinet-style gap, or it is fiction and ZS is not a position, it is a wish.

AMD is the clearest example of buying revenue instead of earning it. It put **$1B of cash into Meta's equity offering** and issued warrants for **up to 160 million shares at $0.01** — 9.8% of the 1.63B share count (stockanalysis) — that vest on GPU delivery targets, and the stock is +13.9% in five sessions to a **$944B market cap on 148x trailing earnings and 112x free cash flow**. A supplier paying its customer in its own equity to take product is not demand; it is a financing arrangement wearing a revenue line. A DCF sees $1B of cash leaving, ~10% of future claims being given away, and a customer whose purchases are now partly self-funded — momentum sees a $1B headline and a Meta logo.

Alphabet is the mirror image: **25% revenue growth, Cloud revenue doubling, and -9.7%** because net income fell 25% and capex is still rising. The market just told you that at a 5% risk-free rate it will not pay for growth that has to be bought with capital. That is a DCF conclusion arriving in public, one name at a time — GOOGL -9.7%, NOW -18%, FTNT -26% — while the index sits 1.28% from an all-time high and VIX sits at 16.91. The divergence that matters most: **HYG was flat and LQD fell 0.22%** through all of it. Credit is not confirming equity fear. That means this is idiosyncratic repricing, not a systemic event — which is worse for you, because there is no Fed put for a two-cent EPS miss. Nobody is coming to rescue a stock. They will just sell it.

RDDT at $134.70 is 3.5% from a $130 alert that fires **tonight**, after a print where consensus needs 50% revenue growth to hold. It is already 26% below where it traded at its last report ($180.90) and 40% down YTD, with a 52-wk low of $119.27 — so a miss gaps straight through $130 and your "entry" gets filled at a price nobody tested. That is not a margin of safety; that is a market order disguised as a limit. And AMZN at $254.60 with a ±7.4% implied move tonight and a stop at $199: a -7.4% gap takes it to ~$236, which is still $6 above the $230 add zone, so the only realistic outcomes are (a) you hold through it or (b) you get to average down. Neither is a plan you should be discovering at 4:30pm.
