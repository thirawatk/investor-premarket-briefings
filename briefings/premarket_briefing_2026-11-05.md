## 🔑 Two Big Stories

**1. The blue sweep reprices AI capex at the state level, not the Fed level.** Democrats took the House and Senate and flipped Texas, Ohio and Georgia — the three states where the AI buildout physically happens. Goldman's estimate that ~45% of planned US data-center capacity is exposed to local permitting is the single most important number printed this week; Dan Ives' counter is that only 5–10% of sites actually fail. The market split the difference instantly: chips down (NVDA −3.2%, MU −5%, CoreWeave slammed), hyperscalers up (AMZN +6%, MSFT +6.7%) on the "drawbridge" logic — existing capacity becomes a moat. Note what that means: the same capex number is bullish for AWS and bearish for the companies selling into AWS.

**2. Oil's 8% collapse is doing the Fed's work — for now.** WTI settled $88.27 (−8.1%) and Brent $94.80 (−8.5%) as US–Iran talks through Qatari mediators advanced, with Trump himself conceding the Iran war cost the GOP the midterms. That is a −14% week in crude, and it is the only reason the 10Y fell 5.8 bp to 4.894% while the Dow gained 3%. But gold +3.5% to $4,080 on the same day tells you the dollar/fiscal hedge bid never left — this was not a clean risk-on; it was a disinflation headline plus a weaker dollar.

## 🧠 The Hard Truth (Damodaran Lens)

Yesterday the market repriced a multi-trillion-dollar AI capex program off a *gubernatorial* result. Either local permitting permanently clips 5–10% of planned capacity — in which case 2028–2030 terminal cash flows at NVDA/AVGO/AMD are lower and the multiple compression hasn't even started — or it doesn't, and yesterday's chip selloff was free money. You cannot hold both views, and at $200 NVDA the tape is holding neither: it's down 3% on a headline that arguably *helps* pricing power.

The rates picture is where the margin of safety is thinnest. The Fed is at 3.88% with a **71% probability of a hike** on Dec 9, the 10Y is 4.894% and the 30Y is 5.28%. Equities are rallying on gridlock and cheaper oil — a disinflation trade that requires Iran talks to keep producing headlines and requires the long end to ignore a 5.28% term premium. Gold at $4,080 says the bond market's fiscal problem did not go away because the Democrats won a Senate seat.

Your book shows the other side of the same coin. You are paying for capex discipline you don't control.
