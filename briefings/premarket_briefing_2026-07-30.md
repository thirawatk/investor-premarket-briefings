## 3. Key Themes

### 🔥 **Iran Attack Escalation — Oil Surges, Risk-Off Ripples**
Iran launched surprise missile attacks against US forces on Wednesday, and Saudi Arabia carried out strikes against Iran-aligned militias in Iraq. Brent crude jumped 6.45% to $89.51/bbl and WTI surged 6.75% to ~$84.61. This marks the most significant escalation since the US-Iran strikes in early July. The Strait of Hormuz remains the critical tail-risk lever — a sustained blockade would push oil into triple-digit territory and trigger global stagflation fears. Markets are pricing a "rolling crisis" premium rather than full-scale war, but the peace process has been completely derailed. Energy stocks (XLE) will be the key sector to watch today.

### 🏦 **Fed Holds at 3.50%-3.75% — Warsh's Data-Dependent Pivot**
The FOMC held rates steady on July 29 as widely expected, with 62% of market participants pricing a hold. Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized a data-dependent approach, noting that while CPI cooled to 3.5% annual (down from 4.2% in May), inflation remains significantly above the 2% target. Warsh's shift away from forward guidance towards a more reactive stance adds uncertainty. Markets still price ~38% odds of a hike later this year. The Q2 GDP advance estimate of 4.1% annualized growth provides the Fed room to stay patient, but rising oil prices could reignite inflation expectations.

### 📊 **Meta Crushes Earnings — AI Spending Narrative Strengthened**
Meta Platforms reported Q2 2026 results after the bell that stunned Wall Street: EPS of $7.14 vs $5.89 expected (21% beat), revenue of $47.5B vs $44.8B expected (6% beat), and advertising revenue of $46.5B vs $44.07B expected. Reality Labs losses narrowed to $4.5B vs $4.8B expected. The company guided Q3 revenue to $47.5B-$50.5B, well above the $46.2B Street estimate. Shares jumped ~12% in after-hours trading. CFO Susan Li noted infrastructure costs are the largest driver of expense growth, with the company investing "hundreds of billions" in AI data centers including the 5-gigawatt Hyperion facility. This validates the massive AI capex thesis.

However, the oil price surge threatens to reignite inflation and could slow growth in Q3. The Employment Cost Index (due today at 8:30 AM ET) will provide the latest read on wage inflation, a key input for the Fed's rate decision calculus.

Oil has risen 20%+ since the conflict began. The key risk is a sustained disruption to the Strait of Hormuz, through which ~20% of global oil transits. Markets are now pricing a structural geopolitical risk premium across equities, bonds, and commodities. This is not a one-day event — it's a regime shift.

## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is caught in a triple bind that no valuation model can cleanly resolve.**

First, **the AI capex arms race has reached a point of diminishing marginal signaling.** Meta just reported a blowout quarter, yet the market's reaction to "we're spending hundreds of billions on AI data centers" is now a known variable. The question is no longer "are they spending?" but "when does the return on invested capital justify the expenditure?" At 27x forward earnings for Amazon and 30x+ for Meta, the market is pricing in perfection. The SK Hynix miss is a canary — it suggests that the hardware buildout may be outpacing the actual AI revenue monetization curve. Damodaran would note that the "growth at any price" mentality is alive and well, but the terminal value assumptions embedded in these valuations require flawless execution for years.

Second, **oil at $85+ with a geopolitical risk premium is the worst possible combination for a market already worried about inflation.** The Fed just held rates because CPI cooled to 3.5%, but oil-driven inflation could reverse that progress within a quarter. The 10-year yield at 4.65% and the 30-year touching 5.16% — the highest since July 2007 — tells you the bond market is screaming about fiscal sustainability and inflation risk. The equity market hasn't fully priced this in because it's been mesmerized by AI earnings. But when the discount rate rises, terminal values shrink, and no amount of revenue growth can compensate for a permanently higher cost of capital.

Third, **the concentration risk in the S&P 500 is extreme and fragile.** The top 10 stocks make up more than 40% of the index. When the Magnificent Seven report, the entire market moves on their results. This is not diversification — it's a leveraged bet on a handful of companies executing flawlessly in a rising-rate, rising-oil, rising-geopolitical-risk environment. The VIX at 20 is telling you that the market knows this, even if it hasn't acted on it yet.

**The honest assessment:** The market is pricing in a Goldilocks scenario where AI monetization accelerates, the Fed cuts rates, oil prices stabilize, and geopolitical tensions de-escalate. The probability of ALL four happening simultaneously is low. The risk-reward at current levels is asymmetric to the downside. A value investor today would be building a watchlist, not chasing momentum.
