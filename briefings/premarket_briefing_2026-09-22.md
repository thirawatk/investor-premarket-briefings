## 🔑 Two Big Stories

**1. The 1999 Breadth Divergence — S&P at ATH But Under the Hood, It's Ugly**

The S&P 500 rallied to within 0.7% of its all-time high on Monday, but here's what CNBC flagged: **30 stocks hit new 52-week lows while only 7 hit new highs.** The last time the index rallied 1%+ to within 1% of a 52-week high while new lows outnumbered new highs was **December 21, 1999** — three months before the dotcom bubble popped. The rally is being carried by a handful of names (Meta, AMD, NVDA) while the median stock is struggling. This is the textbook signature of a narrowing market topping pattern. The index looks healthy; the average stock does not.

**2. Oil's Iran Collapse — $10 Crashed Out on Diplomatic Hopes**

WTI crude fell ~$10/bbl over the past week to $92.28 as Saudi Arabia boosted exports through the Strait of Hormuz and fresh diplomatic signals emerged between Washington and Tehran. Iranian officials are expected to meet country leaders in New York this week. Trump threatened to "wipe out Iran" while simultaneously signaling openness to talks. The oil drop is a massive inflation relief for consumers and tech (lower energy input costs, lower inflation expectations), but the war risk remains binary — any breakdown in negotiations could send crude back above $100 overnight.
