## 3. Key Themes

- **📉 The bond market rescue mission — Bessent doubles debt buybacks.** With the 30-year at its highest since 2007 and the 10-year near 5%, Treasury announced it will double its buyback program to relieve funding pressure — sending yields sharply lower and stocks higher. Skeptics abound: former CEA chair Jason Furman warned Treasury "can't change the underlying fundamentals" of the curve, and UBS sees the opportunity in the 2–5 year segment. This is fiscal QE-adjacent debt management: supportive for risk in the short run, a yellow flag for dollar and inflation credibility long-run.

- **🛢️ Iran war + Hormuz = energy shock that won't quit.** The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed/contested with Iran demanding major US concessions; talks are stalled and the UAE has severed trade with Iran after a reported missile strike. Brent $92.56 (+1.7%), WTI at a three-week high (+2.3%), nat gas +2.4%. Oil is up ~37% YoY and is the single biggest threat to the Fed's inflation math (CPI already 3.3%). Every headline from the Gulf is a live market-mover today.

- **🏛️ The Fed is a 9–3 committee with a hawkish minority.** Yesterday's FOMC minutes confirmed Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all wanted a *hike* at the July 28–29 meeting (rates held at 3.50–3.75%, prime 6.75%). ## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

Step back from the 1% bounces and look at what you're actually paying for. The S&P 500 sits ~1% below record highs with a war on the world's most important oil chokepoint, headline CPI at 3.3% (target: 2%), negative payroll growth, a 30-year Treasury at 5.2%, and a Federal Reserve so split it needed a 9–3 vote just to hold. That is not the backdrop that historically justifies peak multiples — it is a market paying 26–28x earnings for three things to all go right: a soft landing, an uninterrupted AI capex supercycle, and a peaceful Hormuz. Value investors call that a "trinity of hope," and each leg is individually fragile. Nvidia at $219 (33.6x trailing, ~24x FY26 estimates) is the cleanest expression: the consensus already embeds $394B FY26 revenue (+82%) and $562B in FY27 — at $5.3T market cap, the stock is priced for perfection in a sector whose own equipment suppliers just fell 6% in a day. Moderna is the other extreme: $142 against a $42 analyst target is the market paying for a decade of optionality in a single afternoon — wonderful science, terrible margin of safety. Bessent's buybacks don't change Furman's point: you cannot buy your way out of fiscal fundamentals, and the Fed's 3.50–3.75% rate against 4.3% one-year inflation expectations is a negative real-rate gamble that only pays if the war ends and inflation breaks. Buy quality, demand a margin of safety, and remember: in a market this expensive, the price you pay *is* the risk you take.
