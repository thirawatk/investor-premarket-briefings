## 🧠 The Hard Truth (Damodaran Lens)

The S&P's CAPE just hit its highest since the dot-com bubble (Motley Fool, Sunday) while the risk-free rate sits at 5.18% — an earnings yield near ~2.5% against a 5.2% risk-free number is a **negative equity risk premium on trailing earnings**. Every dollar of assumed AI-driven growth is being paid for in advance, and the discount rate is moving against you: this desk called AMD "fully priced" at $543; it closed Friday at $630, +12.6% on the week, with nothing in the cash flows changing except the cost of capital going *up*. The divergences are the tell: the average company is being priced down (Russell −0.8% on the week, XLE −3.5%, XLU −3.9%, LQD −1.4%) while the index is dragged higher by the AI capex complex (SOX +6.3%) — and the VIX at 14.87 says nobody is hedging the rates risk at all. What perfection requires: AI capex converting to durable high-ROIC cash flows on schedule (MU Wednesday is the check), oil staying in the $90s despite Hormuz being shut in all but name, and the Fed's hiking cycle ending before it bites demand. ZS's −10% single session on a **CRO resignation** is the object lesson in thin margins of safety: at these multiples, personnel news costs a tenth of per-share value overnight. A DCF investor at r≈9–10% cost of equity can't underwrite this tape without assuming near-flawless execution — so the honest question per share is not "is AI real?" (it is) but "what am I paying today for earnings I won't see for three years, and what happens if the third year slips?"
