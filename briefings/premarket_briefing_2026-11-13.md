## 🧠 The Hard Truth (Damodaran Lens)

The S&P sits 3.2% from an all-time high with VIX at 18.7 while the risk-free rate is 5.10% and the next Fed move is a hike — that combination only makes sense if you believe earnings growth outruns a rising discount rate indefinitely. Run the arithmetic: at 5.10% the equity risk premium on a ~5.5% forward earnings yield is roughly zero to negative. You are not being paid for owning a claim junior to a Treasury that now pays you 5.1% with certainty. That is not a forecast; it is the price you are being asked to accept.

NVDA is the cleanest example of the gap. Simply Wall St's DCF puts intrinsic value near **$312** against a **$166** price and a 21x trailing P/E — a "48% undervalued" headline that requires the model to assume flawless multi-year execution by an organization that currently has no permanent chief executive. Every dollar of that DCF rests on roadmaps, allocation decisions and hyperscaler relationships that flowed through one person. What a DCF investor should notice: the model's terminal value is a management-quality assumption wearing a spreadsheet costume. Cheaper is not the same as cheap when the asset you are underwriting is a person who just left. And the rotation into AMD and AVGO (+44.7% and +25% in five sessions) is the market paying up for *diversification away from* that single point of failure at 40x+ forward earnings — it is swapping concentration risk for valuation risk, not eliminating risk.
