## 🔑 Two Big Stories

**1. The Huang bounce is fully erased, and NVDA is now priced as a cyclical.**
NVDA closed $149.30, −3.91%, giving back the entire +31% / $900B rally from the Nov 23 board reinstatement. This happened despite a genuinely strong Nov 17 print: Q3 FY27 revenue **$115.8B (+103% y/y)**, EPS $2.68 vs $2.47, Q4 guided to **$130B ±3%**, and FY28 growth *raised* to **75% from 70%** (NVIDIA IR). At $149.30 and FY28 consensus EPS of $15.73, NVDA trades at **9.5x next year's earnings and 16.1x this year's** — a multiple the market normally reserves for a business whose revenue is about to flatten. That is the whole argument in one number: the market is no longer paying for growth, it is paying for the *probability that $690B of FY28 revenue is a peak*. Adding pressure: Q4 gross margin guided down to **71–72%** from 74.9%, which management called "the bottom" — but in a value framework, a 300bp margin give-back plus export controls is the first crack in pricing power, not a footnote. Also note the politics: Trump discussed invoking wartime powers to force Huang's reinstatement (WaPo, via @marketfeed). A CEO installed by the state is a governance discount, not a catalyst.

**2. Trump's 14-country tariff salvo lands on top of a 3.3% core PCE — the Fed is about to hike into it.**
USTR proposed tariffs on 14 partners, notably a **20% excess-capacity levy on China** stacked on existing ~25% Section 301 duties, plus Japan and the EU, effective early 2027. This is the administration's replacement vehicle after the Supreme Court struck down the sweeping IEEPA tariffs. Same morning, **core PCE came in at 3.3% y/y vs 3.2% expected, the hottest since Nov 2023**. The market's read is unambiguous: **CME FedWatch/Kalshi/Frenzy all cluster at 94–96% for a 25bp HIKE at the Dec 8–9 FOMC**. So the setup for the next six weeks is: an AI growth scare, a Fed tightening, and a fresh tariff regime that raises input costs *while* demand decelerates. That is textbook bad for long-duration equities — and yet the 10Y *fell* 6bp on the day, which tells you the bond market is buying the growth scare over the inflation shock. One of those two markets is wrong.
