## 3. Key Themes

### 🔥 Hormuz Deal Rally — The Biggest Catalyst in Months
The Dow's 900-point Tuesday explosion was driven by reports that the US, Iran, and Oman are "close" to a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which Iran has blockaded since late February following the US-Israeli military campaign. Treasury Secretary Bessent signaled a deal could come "soon," and Trump claimed the strait could "fully open soon." This would remove a massive overhang on global energy supply, sending oil lower and risk assets higher. **The rally is real but priced in — watch for "sell the news" if a deal actually materializes.**

July ISM Services PMI came in at 54.1, below the 54.5 consensus and barely above June's 54.0. S&P Global's PMI was stronger at 54.6 (8-month high). **The services sector is the backbone of the US economy — no recession signal, but no acceleration either.**

The Fed Funds rate sits at 3.63%, a full 105bp below the Taylor Rule's implied 4.68%. The Fed is effectively running an accommodative policy despite above-target inflation. The rally is driven by: (1) Hormuz uncertainty creating inflation fears, (2) the prospect of a deal easing rate hike bets, (3) central bank buying, and (4) a weak dollar below DXY 100. **Gold is telling you the market doesn't trust the "soft landing" narrative completely.**

## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is celebrating a Hormuz deal that doesn't exist yet.** The S&P 500 is at record highs, the Dow crossed 54,000 for the first time, and the Nasdaq jumped 2.6% in a single session — all on the *hope* that a diplomatic deal will reopen a strait that's been blockaded for five months. This is narrative-driven investing at its finest, and it's dangerous.

**The fundamentals don't support euphoria.** The Fed Funds rate is 105bp below what the Taylor Rule says it should be. That's not "accommodative" — that's artificially stimulative. Meanwhile, the 10-year yield at 4.63% is telling you the bond market doesn't believe inflation is conquered. The ISM Services PMI at 54.1 is fine but not accelerating. Labor productivity forecasts are tepid. Unit labor costs are rising. This is an economy running on monetary stimulus and geopolitical hope, not organic strength.

**AMD's earnings reaction is the tell.** Revenue up 50%, data center doubled, EPS up 156% — and the stock drops 6%. This is what happens when valuations price in perfection. The S&P 500 is trading at elevated multiples, and the bar for earnings beats is now so high that genuine operational excellence gets punished. When "better than expected" isn't enough, you're in a momentum market, not a value market.

**Gold at $4,300 is the real signal.** If the economy were truly in a "soft landing" with controlled inflation, gold wouldn't be surging. The market is hedging against: (1) geopolitical tail risks that haven't been resolved, (2) inflation that's stickier than the Fed admits, and (3) a dollar that's losing credibility. DXY below 100 with yields at 4.6% is unusual — it suggests foreign buyers are rotating out of Treasuries.

**Bottom line:** Enjoy the rally, but don't confuse a relief bounce with a new bull market. The Hormuz deal is a catalyst for a 2-3% move, not a regime change. If productivity data disappoints today or the FOMC minutes reveal hawkish dissent, the market will give back Tuesday's gains faster than it earned them.
