Nothing about Nvidia's cash flows, backlog or roadmap changed between Friday's close and Monday's — what changed is the market's estimate of *execution risk*, which is a confidence variable, not a terminal-value input. That is precisely what makes it reversible: the same board that reversed itself can reverse again, and the reason it had to reverse was political, not operational.

**2. Wednesday's PCE is the real gate, and the market is pricing a December hike as near-certain.** CME FedWatch showed the odds of a 25bp **hike** at the Dec 8–9 meeting rising from 82.6% (Oct 28) to 95% (Nov 17 close) to **above 97%** as of Nov 20 — this is a tightening cycle, not a cut cycle. Yesterday's oil collapse is the one thing that helps: WTI sub-$80 plus diesel easing is the fastest route to a softer print, and Saxo's Hansen flags exactly that the refined-fuel relief "could help alleviate inflation pressures, and in turn, reduce concerns about further rate hikes." The rally is therefore a bet on a data point nobody has seen yet.

## 🧠 The Hard Truth (Damodaran Lens)

The S&P added 326 points, or roughly $1.6 trillion of market cap, because a board un-fired a CEO. In a DCF that changes no revenue line, no margin, no reinvestment rate — it changes the *probability you assign to the plan being executed*, which is a confidence discount, not a cash flow. Any index that can move 4.73% on a governance reversal was not priced off discounted cash flows to begin with; it was priced off a narrative with a lever attached. So ask the uncomfortable question the momentum crowd skipped: what is the market pricing in? It is pricing a soft landing, an AI capex boom with no digestion phase, and a Fed that stops at 4.00%. You may have at most two of those. With Williams openly saying he would hike at consecutive meetings and FedWatch at 97% for December, the third assumption is already failing. The credit market agrees with me, not with the tape: HYG +0.25% and LQD +0.23% against SPX +4.73% is the signature of a multiple re-rating, not an improvement in default expectations or the risk-free rate — if this were a genuine discount-rate event, high yield would have ripped. And the VIX falling 26% in a day is not risk being *retired*, it's risk being *repriced downward on one headline*. The thinnest margin of safety in your book is nowhere near the AI complex — it's AMZN at $255.39, 1.6% under your $259.55 cost with the add zone still $25 away, which is the only name where a real MoS test is still live. The widest gap between price and value is CRCL: two of your three exit conditions have already printed (share price $8.66 below $60, USDC at $69.0B below $70B) and you are still holding. Circle's economics are a distribution arrangement, not a moat, and a stablecoin losing supply to USDT — $69B vs $174.66B — is losing the only war that matters. Finally, look at AMD: yesterday it filed an S-3ASR covering **336,934,324 shares for resale, roughly 20.3% of its 1,656,317,509 shares outstanding**, including 160M warrants each to OpenAI and Meta at a **$0.01 exercise price**, vesting on GPU-purchase milestones and share-price thresholds up to **$600**. Anyone valuing AMD per share on today's share count is understating dilution by a fifth, and at $453.46 the market is already paying for warrant vesting that hasn't happened.
