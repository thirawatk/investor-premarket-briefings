## 3. Key Themes

### 🔴 **Fed Holds, But Hawks Are Circling**
The Fed held rates at 3.50%–3.75% for the fifth consecutive meeting on July 29, but **three officials dissented in favor of a hike** — a rare split. PCE inflation cooled to 3.7% YoY in June (from 4.1% in May), but remains nearly double the 2% target. Fed Governor Hammack stated she "lacked confidence" inflation would return to target on its own. The next FOMC decision is September 16 — markets are pricing a meaningful probability of a hike. **This is the most hawkish hold in years.**

### 📉 **Q2 GDP: Weak Headline, Strong Underlying**
Q2 GDP came in at 1.5%, badly missing the 2.1% consensus — dragged down by a widening trade deficit and inventory drawdowns. But **real final sales to domestic purchasers surged 3.9%**, a major acceleration that tells a very different story. Consumer spending and AI-driven capex remain robust. The headline miss gives doves ammunition, but the demand surge gives hawks theirs. Net-net: the economy is growing, just not in the way the GDP deflator measures cleanly.

### 🏭 **Manufacturing Day — ISM & PMI in Focus**
Today's key data: S&P Global Final Manufacturing PMI (9:45 AM ET / 8:45 PM BKK) and ISM Manufacturing PMI (10:00 AM ET / 9:00 PM BKK). ISM Manufacturing was 53.3 in June (expansion for 6th straight month). Chicago PMI surged to 57.6 in July, signaling manufacturing momentum. Watch for any deceleration — it would confirm the GDP softness.

Motley Fool flagged a striking historical pattern: since 1950, the S&P 500 has suffered an **average peak-to-trough decline of 18% during midterm election years**, with corrections typically bottoming in August. 2026 is a midterm year. With the S&P at 7,437 and RBC's target at 8,150, the market has room — but also vulnerability. The "Leopold liquidation" in late July (a forced unwind of momentum positions) was a taste of what August volatility can look like.

## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is paying growth-stock multiples for a value-stock problem.**

At 4.73%, the 10-year Treasury yield is telling you that the risk-free rate is not coming down. Three Fed dissents tell you the next move might be UP. PCE at 3.7% tells you inflation is not conquered — it's sticky, it's supply-driven (energy, Iran), and the Fed knows it.

Yet the S&P 500 is trading at forward multiples that assume rates normalize. AMD at 156x earnings. Palantir at 139x. SpaceX trading below its IPO price because the market is starting to price in reality: these are great companies at terrible prices.

The Q2 GDP headline (1.5%) was ugly, but the 3.9% private demand surge means the economy doesn't need a rate cut. It needs the Fed to hold. And holding at 3.75% with 3.7% inflation means **real rates are barely positive** — still accommodative. The hawks are right to dissent.

**What Damodaran would say:** The equity risk premium is being compressed by AI euphoria while the risk-free rate rises. That's a dangerous combination. When the ERP compresses and rates rise simultaneously, you get multiple compression — eventually. The market can stay irrational longer than you can stay solvent, but the math is unforgiving. At these levels, you're paying for perfection in an imperfect world with a hot war in the Middle East and a Fed that can't cut.

**The honest take:** If you're buying AMD at $476 for the AI story, you need revenue growth of 30%+ for the next 3 years to justify that price. That's possible. But it's not free. And any earnings miss this week will remind the market of that.
