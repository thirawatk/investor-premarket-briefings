## 3. Key Themes

- **The bond market is the whole ballgame.** Long-term yields hit pre-2008-crisis highs this week on ballooning deficits, a mountain of AI-related corporate borrowing, and war-driven oil. Every equity move of consequence this week was downstream of yields. Friday's rally happened simply because yields "stopped lurching."

- **Bessent's buyback gambit bought 24 hours, not credibility.** Doubling long-dated buybacks (starting Sept 9, through early November, funded by bill issuance — not QE) initially sent yields and the dollar down and even spiked Bitcoin to $78K on yield-curve-control speculation. But the relief faded within a day; by Friday the 10Y was back at 4.736%. The market is telling Treasury the term-premium problem is bigger than $4B operations.

- **Nvidia earnings Wednesday is the week's fulcrum.** NVDA ($214.72, mkt cap $5.2T, P/E ~33) reports fiscal Q2 after the close Aug 26. Street expects ~+99% EPS y/y on ~+97% revenue growth; FY27 consensus revenue is ~$395B (+83%). Analysts overwhelmingly bullish (49 Strong Buy / 10 Buy / 2 Hold / 1 Strong Sell; avg PT $304.73, +42% upside; fresh targets: BMO initiate $340, Benchmark $335, Jefferies/RBC $300). Top-5 hyperscalers are now expected to nearly double capex in 2026 (Susquehanna). TipRanks sentiment: 48.9% bullish / 47.9% neutral / just 3.2% bearish.

- **The consumer is cracking at the margins — Walmart edition.** Walmart posted its slowest US sales growth in years and fell >9%, its worst day since 2022, erasing tens of billions in market value (it did raise its earnings outlook on cost control). Klarna separately cut GMV and revenue guidance. Beaten-down consumer reads matter more than usual with core PCE at 3.3%.

- **Warsh Fed = asymmetric policy risk.** Rates have been on hold since the December 2025 cut; Warsh (sworn in May, first meeting June 17) has flipped the dot-plot hawkish. Prediction markets and CME FedWatch put September at ~74–75% no-change, ~25% HIKE, ~1% cut. A hot July PCE print Friday could push hike odds toward parity. Watch Friday's speech for whether Warsh "addresses the elephant in the room, which is inflation" (ex-Philly Fed chief Harker).

- **US–Iran conflict escalation keeps a floor under oil and gold.** The Islamabad MoU truce expired Aug 17; the US launched a new sanctions campaign ("economic war") over the weekend, and Iran warned any country joining would be treated as an enemy. Trump says Tehran isn't ready for "the right deal." Casualty counts and Gulf-base strikes keep tail risk elevated — this is why WTI holds $87 and gold prints records despite elevated real yields.

- **Crypto traded like a rates asset, not a risk asset.** BTC jumped 5.9% Friday and crypto-linked equities led the tape — Robinhood +13.7%, Coinbase +9.2% — helped by clearer-regulation talks between the administration and industry leaders. Digital gold and physical gold rising together (+2% GLD Friday) is becoming "the new normal," per Motley Fool commentary.

## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

Strip out the noise and this market is asking you to believe two incompatible things at once. First: that AI capex grows into infinity — NVDA trades at 33x trailing earnings against consensus FY28 EPS of $12.96, which prices in revenue nearly tripling from FY26's $216B to $569B within two years. That is not a valuation, that's a prophecy; it leaves zero margin for error in hyperscaler budgets that are themselves funded by an unprecedented wave of AI corporate borrowing. Second: that the inflation problem is solved — yet core PCE sits at 3.3%, the Fed's own chair has turned hawkish, and a quarter of the rates market is pricing a *hike*, while the 30-year at 5.27% represents the longest-end repricing since before the GFC.

The honest math: when your discount rate rises because the government itself can't fund its debt without interventions that look increasingly like stealth yield management, every long-duration cash flow — AI growth stories included — is worth less. Bessent's buyback program didn't fail because it was small; it failed because markets correctly read it as confirmation that the fiscal situation requires managing. Value discipline says: don't pay prophecy prices during a term-premium regime shift. If you own NVDA, own it knowing Wednesday's print must justify a $5.2T market cap with margin expansion, not just top-line beats. And treat gold's melt-up for what it is — not an asset class endorsement, but a referendum on fiscal credibility that also happens to be priced in your equity multiples.
