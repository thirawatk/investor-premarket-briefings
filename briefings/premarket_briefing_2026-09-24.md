The 10-year yield surged to 5.11% on Wednesday — the biggest one-day move in 18 months. The 5-year crossed 5% for the first time since 2007. This wasn't a gradual move: it was a violent repricing driven by oil-inflation fears colliding with a Fed that just hiked 25bps on Sep 16 to 3.75%-4.00%. Markets now price **94.5% odds of another hike** at the Oct 28 FOMC. The bond market is doing what the stock market refuses to: pricing in a Fed that's behind the curve on inflation and willing to keep tightening into a geopolitical oil shock. This is the single biggest risk to every equity position.

**2. Oil Collapses Below $92 as Ceasefire Takes Hold — But the Inflation Damage Is Done**
WTI crude crashed from $100+ to $92 on a 2-week US-Iran ceasefire deal. The Strait of Hormuz — which had seen vessel traffic collapse 95% — is reopening. But here's the catch: the inflation genie is already out of the bottle. Core PCE was elevated, supply chains are still disrupted, and the Fed's own Sep 16 statement acknowledged "economic activity expanding at a solid pace" while hiking. Oil falling is good for consumer wallets, but the rate market doesn't care — it's pricing in the lagged inflation from months of $100+ oil, not the forward path. Energy was the only green sector yesterday; the rest of the market is absorbing the rate shock.
