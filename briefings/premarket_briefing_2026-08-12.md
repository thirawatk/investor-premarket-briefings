## 3. Key Themes

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports July CPI at **8:30 AM ET (7:30 PM BKK)**. Consensus: headline +0.1% MoM / 3.4% YoY; core +0.2% MoM / 2.5% YoY. Goldman Sachs's model calls for a hotter +0.33% core MoM — if that prints, expect an immediate repricing of September Fed hike odds. June CPI surprised to the downside (-0.4% MoM headline, +2.6% core YoY), so the market is positioned for benign data. A hot print would be the kind of shock that re-rates everything. **SeekingAlpha analysts are warning this CPI could come "hot"** and accelerate pressure on the AI trade.

Trump told Axios he's "only semi-negotiating" with Iran, while Iranian FM Araghchi said there's "no possibility of restarting negotiations" under current conditions. Brent crude at ~$88/bbl and WTI at $83/bbl remain elevated, keeping inflationary pressure alive. This is the geopolitical wild card that could turn a mild CPI into a crisis.

The CPI print today will determine whether the hold camp holds or the hawks regain control. The Fed Funds rate sits at **3.50-3.75%** after the July 29 hold.

## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is pricing perfection and hoping for diplomacy.**

Strip away the narrative, and here's what you're actually buying at S&P 500 = 7,732: forward P/E of ~22x on earnings that are growing 50% YoY — but that growth is concentrated in a handful of mega-cap names (Alphabet, Amazon, and the AI complex). Excluding those two, growth is 32%, which is still excellent but not the 50% the headline suggests.

The Damodaran question is simple: **what earnings growth rate are you paying for, and is it sustainable?**

At 22x forward, the market is pricing in ~12-15% earnings growth for the next several years. That's achievable if AI capex translates to real revenue and margins — but CoreWeave has yet to prove it can generate free cash flow, Super Micro's margins are compressed, and Intel is diluting shareholders to fund its turnaround. The $500B Nvidia financing deal is impressive in scale but raises the question: if AI infrastructure is so profitable, why does it need $500B in external financing?

Meanwhile, the geopolitical risk is being systematically underpriced. The VIX at 15.45 says "no problem" — but the Strait of Hormuz carries ~20% of global oil transit. A genuine disruption would send oil above $100, re-rate inflation expectations upward, and force the Fed into the kind of tightening that kills bull markets.

**The honest assessment:** You're paying peak multiples for peak earnings during peak geopolitical uncertainty. That's not automatically wrong — markets can stay expensive for years — but it leaves zero margin for error. Today's CPI is the kind of binary event that separates investors from tourists.
