## 3. Key Themes

**🔴 China's DUV lithography breakthrough just repriced the entire chip complex.** Reports Monday that a state-backed Chinese firm has begun mass-producing domestic DUV lithography machines sent ASML down 7% and NVDA/AMD/SK Hynix/Micron down as much as 9% intraday; equipment names AMAT, LRCX, KLAC fell in sympathy. This is a structural, not cyclical, story: if China can self-supply DUV (the workhorse for mature and mid-node chips), the West's export-control moat narrows and the memory/equipment pricing power thesis weakens. Notably, this hits the same day SK Hynix reports its first earnings since its US listing (Tuesday) — expect heavy focus on China exposure in the call.

**🛢️ The Iran war premium is unwinding faster than it built.** Three straight nights without US strikes on Iran, Trump signaling openness to diplomacy, and Pakistan reportedly pushing to restart talks (with China's backing) knocked ~7% off WTI in one session. But Rystad's Jorge León warns that without a ceasefire, "the risk of a significant rebound in oil prices would be substantial" — the Strait of Hormuz is still constricted, Houthis are still active in the Red Sea, and Russia's refineries remain under Ukrainian bombardment. Treat the oil dip as a truce trade, not a peace trade.

## 5. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is paying for AI infrastructure with cash it doesn't have, and calling it vision.** Alphabet just crossed the Rubicon: negative free cash flow for the first time in its public history, on $200B of planned capex. Deutsche Bank thinks Microsoft's number goes to $238B. Meta wants to spend $650B in 2026. Combined, the hyperscalers will pour ~$724B into capex this year and ~$950B in 2027. Here is the uncomfortable arithmetic: these are no longer asset-light compounders — they are becoming capital-intensive utilities with tech multiples, and the market is valuing them on narratives about 2030 revenue while ignoring the 2026 cash flow statement. When a company trades at 23x earnings (MSFT) but converts zero incremental dollars of capex into provable incremental earnings, you are not investing; you are underwriting someone else's optionality with your capital.

**The Fed hiking into a $4,000 gold price and a $100-oil-scarred economy is telling you the inflation war isn't over — and your discounted cash flow models are lying.** Every DCF run with a 3% terminal growth and a risk-free rate anchored to 2021 assumptions is fantasy. The 10-year at 4.65% with a credible path to 5% means equity risk premiums must compress or earnings must grow faster than they plausibly can. The market fully prices TWO hikes by March and still sits within a few percent of all-time highs. That is not resilience; that is denial.

**And the one genuinely cheap thing on the board — oil peace — is the thing you should trust least.** A three-night pause in a shooting war knocked 7% off crude, but the Strait of Hormuz is still constricted and the Houthis are still firing. If you're buying the dip in equities on the back of falling oil, you are long a ceasefire that doesn't exist. Value is created by cash flows, not headlines. Today, almost nothing in the index is priced for the cash flows it will actually deliver if the Fed hikes twice and oil re-spikes. Act accordingly: demand a margin of safety, because the market is offering none.
